Long Vowels with Fat'hah

– A long vowel is an extension of a short vowel. Arabic language has 3 long vowel letters: ا و ي

– بَ (short) بَا (long)

– بُ (short) بُو (long)

– بِ (short) بِي (long)

– Fat'hah is followed by a naked 'Alif'; eg. بَا

– Dhammah is followed by a naked 'Waw'; eg. بُو

– Kasrah is followed by a naked 'Yaa'; eg. بِي

– Long vowels are lengthened for 2 counts.

Tanwin (Double Vowels)

– Tanwin is the same short vowels accompanying each other.

– It is pronounced by adding an 'n' to the end of the vowel.

– It always comes at the end of a word.

– Two Fathah's will always be accompanied by an 'Alif'. Exceptions are 'Ta mabuta' and 'Hamza'.

Example: بًا (ban), بٌ (bun), بٍ (bin).

Stopping Rules

Stopping rules:

Rules for stopping apply whenever the reciter stops on a word in the middle or at the end of an ayah. Even if the recitation is stopped due to running out of breath, these rules are still applied.

Stopping rules for specific situation are :

Short vowels:

When stopping on a word ending with short vowel, the short vowel is replaced with a sukoon.

Example

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيمِ

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِين

Sometimes when short vowel is removed, it will produce a long vowel.

Example:

هُوَ اللَّهُ الَّذِي لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا هُوَ

لِإِيلَافِ قُرَيْشٍ

Tanwin:

When stopping on a word ending with double dhammah or double kasrah, it is replaced with a sukoon.

Example:

سَيَصْلَى نَارًا ذَاتَ لَهَبٍ

أَيَحْسَبُ أَنْ لَمْ يَرَهُ أَحَدٌ

When stopping on a word ending with double fathah, one fathah is dropped and the word is pronounced with long vowel.

يَقُولُ أَهْلَكْتُ مَالًا لُّبَدًا

ℹ️ How This App Works

📖 READ ARABIC Overview

READ ARABIC is a voice-powered trainer for Arabic letters, vowels, words, and short surahs. Practice your current lesson before advancing.

🎧 Listening Mode (Practice vs Test)

  1. Tap any Arabic letter or word — the app pronounces it aloud with the final Fat'hah, Dhammah, or Kasrah clearly audible. The microphone is not used on tap.
  2. Press Start Test on Lessons 12–14 to turn the microphone on and be scored. The mic is used only while a test is running.
  3. Lessons 1–11 are practice-only — tap any Arabic text to hear it read aloud.
  4. Lessons 3–11 use the Tajweed stopping rule (Madd al-'Iwad) so the last vowel is lengthened for teaching clarity.
  5. Lesson 14 (Recite Ayah) covers Surahs 112, 113, and 114.
  6. On every Start Test (Lessons 12–14), the target must be heard clearly.
  7. When a phrase has more than one word, every word must be spoken before it counts.

⏱ Per-Word Time Window

Each word gets a fresh time window, tuned to the browser's actual recogniser latency. It calibrates silently from your very first answers.

  • Correct → the cell turns green and the test moves on.
  • Miss the window → the cell turns red and the test moves on automatically.
  • The bar turns red during the final seconds.

⏸ Silence Pause

If no speech is heard for 7 seconds, the app pauses the test and switches the mic off. Press ▶ Continue to resume, or ■ Stop Test to end.

🌐 Browser Requirements

Speech recognition requires Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, or Safari, served over HTTPS or localhost. If you see a "Microphone blocked" warning, allow mic access in your browser settings.

⚠️ Note on some Chinese phones: Certain phones sold in mainland China without Google Play Services have no speech-recognition engine. On those devices the app automatically switches to Practice Mode — you can still tap any Arabic text to hear it read aloud.

🔊 Arabic Voice Setup

If tapping a letter produces no sound, your device may be missing an Arabic Text‑to‑Speech voice. The app now detects this automatically and will show you a setup guide. You can also open it manually from the menu ⋮ → Arabic Voice Setup, or tap the button below.

🔊 Set Up Arabic Voice

Your device does not currently have an Arabic Text‑to‑Speech voice installed. Without it, tapping Arabic letters will produce no sound.

  1. Open your phone's Settings app.
  2. Go to System → Languages & input → Text‑to‑speech output (on Samsung: General management → Language and input → Text‑to‑speech).
  3. Tap the ⚙️ gear icon next to Speech Services by Google (or your preferred TTS engine).
  4. Tap Install voice data.
  5. Select العربية (Arabic) and download it.
  6. Return to the app and tap any Arabic letter — you should now hear the pronunciation.

On some devices the path may differ slightly. If you cannot find the TTS settings, try the button below to jump directly to the system TTS screen.
